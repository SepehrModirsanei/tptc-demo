Whites.

The C Whites Style Guide: quiet like tennis whites, drawn on court lines, quick on its feet.

  1. Two grounds and a line: Whites for the club, Blazer navy for High Performance and the club’s close, and the club red kept for the net and for Register.
  2. One ornament, the net: a red line with a post at each end. It is the seam between words and photograph, it divides Recreational from High Performance, and it folds the founding year, 19 on our side and 51 on the court.
  3. Spectral, light and large, speaks for the club on every page, High Performance included, where it turns italic; Schibsted Grotesk carries everything you operate.
  4. Motion arrives like a first serve: masked lines, the net drawing itself, calm reveals, and the High Performance door turning into that page’s hero.
  5. Nothing invented: every fact is sourced, every missing one is a designed Club to confirm slot, and every interim photograph says so.

The net

One red line.

The site’s one ornament: a thin club-red line with a post at each end, used in three places only, so it always means the same thing. Nothing else from a court is drawn as decoration, no baselines, tramlines or service boxes, so the one line carries weight. The full court plan appears only inside an empty photograph frame, where it shows which shot belongs there.

Court planITF dimensions, metres. The net and its posts are the club red here, and only here, as a reference.

The folded year

1951

Home’s hero: 19 on Whites, 51 on the photograph, the net between. On a phone the net stays standing and the year folds across its foot.

The seam

Between words and photograph in both heroes, and between Recreational and High Performance on Home. On a phone the two sides stay side by side, so the first screen holds the headline, the photograph, the actions and the first choice.

Colour

Whites and Blazer. Red for the net.

Two grounds and one line: tennis white for the club, blazer navy for High Performance, and the club’s red kept for the net and for Register now, so red always means the same thing. Six named colours. Dome is the cold light under the Dome in winter, the one secondary surface. Ball is a dot on things that are running today. No grey ground, no gold, no beige. Type

Two voices, seven sizes.

Spectral, a light serif, speaks for the club in every headline, and Schibsted Grotesk, a plain sans, carries everything you press, scan or fill in. Spectral is never bold, and turns italic for High Performance. Both typefaces are open source, under the SIL Open Font License, and are served from the club’s own site, with no outside font service. Figures are lining; columns of prices and times line up.

Train with purpose. Compete with confidence.

Space

An 8px grid, generous at the edges.

Room around things is what makes a page read as a club and not a notice board. Sections breathe 64px on a phone and up to 144px on a desktop. Content is 1344px at most, set in 12 columns with 16, 24 or 48px gutters. Corners are 2px everywhere; there are no card shadows.

Motion

Arrive like a first serve.

Things arrive quickly and settle calmly, like a first serve and its follow-through, and each movement happens once. Three easing curves and a short set of durations. Only position and opacity move, so nothing shifts the layout. With reduced motion switched on, every page is complete and still, and a change of page is a 180ms fade.

Pulse, the pre-serve bounce

The live dot is the ball. It does what a ball does before play: three bounces at the baseline, pushed down, flattened for an instant on the court, back up to the hand, then still (about 2s, under the WCAG 2.2.2 five second limit). It replays once on hover or focus.

Buttons and links

Every state, both grounds.

Each says where it goes, and only Register now is red, so the one red button on any page is always the way to register. A page’s own action is ink, and on hover it opens to an ink outline. It never turns red, on hover or at any other time. A second action is an ink outline. Links carry a 1px underline at rest and draw a heavier line on hover. Focus is a 2px ring, offset 3px, in ink on Whites and in Dome on Blazer.

Status chips

Ball means now.

A small label that says where something stands today: running, opening, closed, full or waiting on a date. The ball dot appears only on a live status (Running now on Home, Under the Dome now on High Performance), worked out in the browser from the club’s published dates, so nothing claims to be live without one. At most one bounces in any view. Every other status is an outline chip; a missing date is a dashed chip with an empty ring.

Club to confirm

A blank, set with care.

The slot marks a fact the club has not yet published, such as a date or a fee, so that nothing on the site is guessed. The page says so, names the missing fact, and gives it the same typographic care as real content: a fill-in line, like a form waiting for a pen.

Photograph frames

Until the photograph arrives.

A frame holds the place of a picture that has not been taken yet, and says exactly which picture belongs there. An empty frame is a court plan drawn the way a court is painted: white lines on Blazer, a lifted navy at night, the net in club red strung to its posts, with the word Placeholder, the frame’s reference and the shot, word for word from the photography brief. Every photograph in this demo is an interim stand-in, so each wears a small Placeholder image tag; the tags come off together when final photography lands. The club’s own photographs carry a dated caption instead.

FAQ

One answer, once.

The questions the club is asked most, in the club’s own words, with one answer in one place. Questions are filed under the club’s topics, the topic in the margin and the question in Spectral beside it. The mark is a plus drawn at court-line weight: when a question opens, its stem lifts and the line it stood on stays, a minus. The answer fades in, rises 8px and ends on the one link that takes you further. Answers come from the club’s lessons FAQ and club policies.

Icons

Drawn like court lines.

Small drawings that mark a kind of thing, a court, a coach, a date, each drawn the way a court line is painted: one weight, square ends.

One family on a 24px grid with a 2px margin: one 1.5px stroke, square-cut ends and mitred corners like painted court lines; straight lines run level, upright or at 45 degrees, and curves appear only where the thing itself is round.

Logo

One logo, drawn one way.

The club’s own: the letters TPTC, the lit yellow tennis ball with its white seam, and the club’s name. It is placed as artwork, redrawn faithfully from the club’s current logo, and it is the same in all three directions and on the full site. Only the letters change with the ground: club red on light, white on dark. The ball never changes: never an outline, never red, never a flat disc. Where there is less than 200 pixels of room, such as a phone header, the short form (TPTC and the ball) takes over. These files stand in until the club supplies its master artwork.

The first screen

The whole picture, before you scroll.

What you see before you touch anything: what the page is, the one thing to do next, the key points, and the whole photograph with its whole subject. This holds on a phone and on a desktop. Nothing in the opening view is cut by the bottom of the screen, and the player is never cropped: racquet, both feet and the shadow on the court are all inside the frame.

Page sections

Name first, then the words.

The larger parts of Home and High Performance, built the same way as on the full site, so the two read as one design. A heading is always larger than the line under it. Every picture slot is filled, with a photograph or a labelled placeholder. Nothing on a page leads with something that is already over.

Two sides of the net
Home’s split. The program name is the heading, in capitals and larger than its three words beneath (Learn. Play. Improve. and, in italic, Train. Compete. Develop.). Each half carries a photograph toward the net: Recreational on the right of its words, High Performance on the left.
Why TPTC, the slideshow
One picture at a time: the club’s two Majors photographs, two labelled placeholders and a marked slot for a club film. Under it: previous, a dot for each slide, next and pause, every one a 44px target; the arrow keys move it too. It moves on by itself every six seconds, never when the visitor asks for reduced motion, and it waits while a pointer or the keyboard is on it or it is off screen. Without script it is a strip you can swipe.
What’s happening
Led by what is running now, the indoor season under the Dome. Each card carries its last day; the browser hides a card once that day has passed, so a session that has ended is never shown as news.
Which step, the finder
High Performance asks two questions, the player’s age and where their game is now, then shows one card: the step, its gate, three facts with icons, Head Pro Josh Borenstein linked to Our Coaches, and the first action. The three steps below stay for browsing.
A page not in this specimen
Links to pages outside this specimen show a short note saying so, instead of doing nothing.
