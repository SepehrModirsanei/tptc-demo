Style Guide, B Dome

Dome

Every colour, typeface, icon and component of this direction, with the reason for each. The idea is one club year under one roof: the year ruled as a single line, the Dome drawn over the indoor months, a serif for the club’s voice, bold scoreboard figures for every number, date, age and price, and one optic yellow, the colour of the ball, that means now.

What sets Dome apart

Dome is the club at night: dark green from edge to edge, a night photograph behind the words, the Dome drawn across it, and every number set like a scoreboard, so it cannot be mistaken for the other two directions.

This direction was revised on 5 October 2026 so that the three directions are clearly three. These are the changes, in plain words.

The photograph
Home now opens on a night photograph, a serve under the steel roof, in place of the forehand by the ivy wall. It is always shown whole: the player is never cut by the edge of the screen, on a phone or on a desktop. High Performance opens on a different night serve, so no photograph appears twice on a page.
The first screen
The photograph stands at the left on the line of the club’s year. The year runs the full width of the screen, May to April, and the Dome rises from October across the photograph. The headline, the two actions and four figures (75 years, 12 months, 4 courts, 19.5 °C) stand under the Dome. All of it fits one screen.
The type
The serif still speaks for the club. Every number, date, age and price is now a bold, narrow scoreboard figure, so the energy of the page is in its type.
The programs
The four programs are four rows on a scoreboard, with no photographs. Choosing High Performance opens that row into the photograph at the head of its page.
The logo
One drawing of the club’s logo everywhere: the letters and the lit yellow ball. Red letters on light grounds, white letters on dark; the ball never changes.
Red
Red is for Register now only. The small red ticks and underlines are now ink.
Contacts
The pages give the club’s general email and the front desk number. The Head Pro’s direct contact is a “Club to confirm” space until the club supplies one.

The logo

The club has one logo and one way of drawing it: the letters and the lit yellow ball with its white seam, so the club looks the same on every page.

Only the letters change with the ground: club red on light, white on dark. The ball is the same drawing everywhere, never an outline, never red. Where there is less than 200 px of room, as in a phone header, the short mark is used without the club’s name beside it.

For the build

The files are in assets/brand: tptc-lockup.svg and tptc-mark.svg for light grounds, the two -on-dark files for dark, favicon.svg for the browser tab. They are placed as images, never redrawn in type. These are faithful redraws of the club’s current logo; the club’s own master files replace them when they arrive.

The season ledger

The club’s year drawn as one ruled line, May to April, with the Dome arched over the indoor months, because playing all year on the same four courts is what sets this club apart.

Four courts play outdoors from about May 1 to September 30, then the Dome goes up and the same courts play indoors until April 25. The arc is the only curve in a square-cornered design, and it stands for one thing: the Dome, over the months it stands. Today is the one bright mark on the line.

The club year. Outdoor courts from about May 1 to September 30. Indoor under the Dome from early October to April 25, 2027.

For the build
  • The dates are the club’s own: outdoor “approximately May 1 to September 30”, indoor “early October 2026 to April 25, 2027”, the Dome “kept at 19.5 celsius”.
  • Today’s mark is worked out in the browser. On the green it is optic yellow; on white it is ink, because yellow on white is too faint to read.
  • The arc rises from the line once, over 1.6 seconds, then today’s mark arrives. With reduced motion it is simply drawn.
  • It appears three times: the Home hero, where it runs the full width of the screen and its arc rises across the photograph; the High Performance introduction (small, with the current session marked); and here. It is never used as decoration.

Colour

Six colours: white paper and dark ink for reading, a deep green for the club at night, a pale green for quiet bands, the club’s red for Register now, and one optic yellow, the colour of a tennis ball, that only ever means something is on now.

Paper #FFFFFF, Ink #0F1A15, Dome night #133126, Dome fabric #ECEFEC, Club red #DF3124 and Optic yellow #CDEB3F. Every pairing of text and ground used on the site is listed below with its measured contrast, so nothing is hard to read.

For the build

The ratios are measured in your browser from the colour values on :root, in whichever theme is showing.

Type

Two typefaces: Newsreader, a serif, is the club’s voice in every headline and sentence, and Archivo sets the small labels and, narrow and bold like a scoreboard, every number, date, age and price, so the energy of the page is in its figures.

Newsreader is set light and never bold, the way a long-established club writes. Archivo is used two ways: wide capitals for labels, like engraved stationery, and narrow, heavy figures for numbers, like the figures on a scoreboard. Every headline, High Performance included, is in the serif; where a headline holds a number, as in “since 1951”, the number is a scoreboard figure.

For the build

Newsreader, variable with optical sizes, weights 340 to 420. Archivo, variable width: labels at width 125, weight 560, tracking 0.16em; figures at width 64, weight 800, tabular lining numerals; a figure inside a sentence is the same face at width 70, weight 700. Both are served from the site itself, with no outside font service.

Spacing and grid

Everything sits on an 8px grid inside twelve columns, so pages line up like ruled paper and keep the same calm at every screen size.

Gutters are 16px on phones and 32px on desktop. Sections are 64px apart on phones and 128px on desktop. On a phone the first screen of a page is a two-column ruled page: the headline on the left, the photograph on the right, the actions and the key points beneath.

For the build

One 4px half step exists for hairline offsets. Content runs to 82rem, full-width bands to 90rem. The phone composition applies under 768px.

Icons

One family of line icons drawn for this club, each taken from real tennis: the court is the true plan of a court, the racquet has strings, and the ball has its seam.

All are drawn on one 24px grid with one 1.5px line, square ends and no fills, so they read as one hand. The ball’s seam is the only S-curve in the family.

Buttons

Three buttons: red for Register now, solid for a page’s main action and an outline for a second action, so red always means the same thing on every page.

Buttons are square and sharp, and each says what it does: Become a member, Book a court, Request an assessment. On hover the button fills; on a press it gives slightly under the finger; the focus ring stays for as long as the button is focused.

For the build

Register now on hover: an ink fill inside the red rule with a white label in both themes, so dark ink never sits on red. Press is a 2% scale. Hover applies on fine pointers only.

Scoreboard rows and program cards

A scoreboard row is one of the four ways into the club’s programs, and a program card is one group lesson with its facts always in the same order, so a parent can compare two at a glance.

The four programs are set as rows on a scoreboard, not as photographs: the row number, the program’s name in the serif, one line, one bold figure with its label, and one way in. The whole row is the link. On a real scoreboard a dot beside a name shows who is serving; here the dot stands beside the row you are on. A program card always reads Who, Level, When, Where, Fee, then the action.

Photographs and placeholders

Until the club’s own photographs are ready, a picture is either an interim photograph tagged “Placeholder image” or a drawn court frame that says what will go there, so nothing is passed off as the club’s own picture.

The drawn frame shows a true court plan, then the word Placeholder, the picture’s reference and one line on what it will show. It is used where no photograph exists yet: the coaches, and the club’s own Dome and courts.

For the build

An interim photograph is read from assets/img/<slot-id>.jpg when the page is built. One switch turns every tag off when final photography lands: remove the ph-tags class from <html> in the head partial.

The live mark

An optic yellow dot marks something that is on now, such as a session in progress, and it appears only when a published date proves it.

The dot is the ball. When a live state first comes into view it is bounced twice, the way a player bounces the ball at the baseline before serving, then it rests. It is always beside words, and there is one live ball on a screen at most.

For the build

The state is worked out in the browser from the published start and end dates. The bounce falls accelerating, flattens on contact, climbs back decelerating and meets its ground shadow at each bounce: about one second in all.

Motion

Every movement on the site copies something real from tennis, the toss, the bounce, a line being laid, the Dome going up, and everything else stays still.

Text and photographs are simply there. Nothing loops, and for visitors who ask their device for reduced motion, nothing moves at all.
