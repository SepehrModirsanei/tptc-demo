The living style guide

Whites.

The style guide for every page of the Thornhill Park Tennis Club website: quiet like tennis whites, drawn on the lines of a court, and quick on its feet. Pages are built from these parts; site/BUILD.md has the copy-paste markup.

  • 01The net
  • 02Colour
  • 03Type
  • 05Motion
  1. Two grounds and a line: Whites for the club, Blazer navy for High Performance and the club’s close, and the club red kept for the net and for Register.
  2. One ornament, the net: a red line with a post at each end. It is the seam between words and photograph, it divides Recreational from High Performance, and it folds the founding year, 19 on our side and 51 on the court.
  3. Spectral, light and large, speaks for the club on every page, High Performance included, where it turns italic; Schibsted Grotesk carries everything you operate.
  4. Motion arrives like a first serve: masked lines, the net drawing itself, calm reveals, and the High Performance door turning into that page’s hero.
  5. Nothing invented: every fact is sourced, every missing one is a designed “club to confirm” slot, and every interim photograph says so.

The net

One red line.

The site has one ornament, and it is the net: a 2px club-red line with a post at each end. It appears in three places only. Everything else that used to be drawn (baselines, tramlines, service boxes, a court-plan footer) was removed, so the one line carries weight. The full court plan survives only inside placeholder frames, where it tells you which shot belongs there.

Court planITF dimensions, metres. The net and its posts are the club red here, and only here, as a reference.

Colour

Whites and Blazer. Red for the net.

Six named values on :root. Whites and Blazer are the two grounds: a tennis white, and the navy of a club blazer and the stripe on a cable-knit tennis sweater. Dome is the cold light of the winter Dome, the one secondary surface. Red is the net and Register. Ball is a dot on things with a real clock behind them. No grey ground, no gold, no beige. Night: the Dome at night

The site follows the visitor’s system setting. At night only the semantic layer changes: the ground becomes the Dome’s fabric after dark, a navy black, never #000; type becomes chalk, never #FFF; photographs drop to 86% brightness so they sit in the room instead of glowing. Type

Two voices, seven sizes.

Spectral (200 to 400, roman and italic) for the club’s voice, never bold, on every page. Schibsted Grotesk (variable) for everything operable and scanned, never for headlines. Space

An 8px grid, generous at the edges.

Sections breathe 64px on a phone and up to 144px on a desktop. Content is 1344px at most, set in 12 columns with 16, 24 or 48px gutters. Radius is 2px everywhere; there are no card shadows.

Motion

Arrive like a first serve.

Three curves, a short set of durations, transform and opacity only. Everything runs once; nothing loops. With reduced motion switched on, every page is complete and still, and a page change is a 180ms fade.

This direction’s own: the net draws itself from one post to the other by scaleY (or scaleX on a phone), 1.1s, once.

Pulse, the pre-serve bounce

The live dot is the ball. It does what a ball does before play: three bounces at the baseline, pushed down, flattened for an instant on the court, back up to the hand, then still (about 2s, under the WCAG 2.2.2 five second limit). It replays once on hover or focus.

Placeholder frames

Until the photograph arrives.

An empty slot is a court plan drawn the way a court is painted: white lines on a Blazer surface (a lifted navy at night), the net in club red strung to its posts, carrying the word Placeholder, the slot id and the shot, word for word from the photography brief. When a file named after the slot lands in assets/img, the photograph covers the frame on its own and wears a small “Placeholder image” tag, because every photograph on the demo is an interim stand-in. One switch turns every tag off when final photography lands: remove the class interim-photos from <html>. Real club photographs carry an archive caption instead.

Icons

Drawn like court lines.

One family on a 24px grid with a 2px margin: one 1.5px stroke, square-cut ends and mitred corners like painted court lines; straight lines run level, upright or at 45 degrees, and curves appear only where the thing itself is round.

Logo lockup

The club’s own mark, redrawn.

A faithful vector redraw of the club’s current logo (assets/brand/, see its README): TPTC drawn letter by letter, the ball with the real tennis-ball seam, the curve that splits a sphere into two identical panels. Serving courts

Four courts, four serves.

Home’s “What are you looking for?” doors, from Direction A, client-approved. Each door is one of the club’s four courts in plan at the true 78 by 36 ft over its photograph, the red net strung post to post. When the section comes into view the courts serve in turn, deuce, ad, deuce, ad, like the first four points of a game; hover or keyboard focus replays a door. Junior: the safe topspin serve a young player learns. High Performance: flat down the T, the fastest. Adult: a slice out wide. Camps: serve and return, like a summer rally. Below 720px a door is a compact row and no ball flies. Reduced motion: each ball rests at its bounce mark, inside the correct box. Marker: <!--@courts /-->. Home only; never a fifth serve.

One question at a time.
